## Bottom line

The three documents propose fifteen sector slots that reduce to ten distinct sectors. Three of the ten appear in all or most documents and pass every manifest requirement: **cybersecurity and software supply-chain assurance**, **grid and energy intelligence**, and the **sovereign cloud and AI software layer**. Two more are proposed by one document each with strong evidence: **industrial AI and robotics** (GPT) and **open-source public-sector software** (Claude). The remaining five are either sub-slices of the first five, blocked by a disagreement the founders must settle (semiconductor design), or outside the digital reading of "tech sovereignty" (agriculture, defence logistics). All three documents agree on the shape of the opportunity: the software layer on top of European infrastructure, never the infrastructure itself. The decisions in §9 are taken: "tech sovereignty" is read as digital only, and the five Tier 1 and Tier 2 sectors (A, B, C, D, G) go forward to exploration.

## 2. The manifest filter

`01-manifest.md` sets four requirements. Applied to every candidate:

1. **European, for Europe.** All ten candidates pass.
2. **Strengthens an under-developed sector and reduces dependence on other markets.** Passes where a document names a measured dependency (cloud share, security-vendor share, AI adoption gap). Fails where the dependency is asserted without a figure (Gemini's grid, agro and defence claims) **[unsourced]**.
3. **Financeable through EU programmes and VC.** All ten name at least one EU instrument. Defence logistics is the exception on the VC side: many European funds exclude defence by mandate **[reviewer]**.
4. **Low capital, strong moat, sustainable model.** Fails for anything that requires owning hardware, data centres, fabs or vehicles. This excludes the heat-reuse and cooling parts of the data-centre sector, chip tape-outs, and swarm hardware. Software-only versions of each pass.

**One ambiguity the manifest leaves open.** "Tech sovereignty" can mean digital technology (GPT and Claude read it this way) or strategic autonomy at large (Gemini reads it this way and includes fertiliser dependence and defence supply chains). The reading decides whether sectors I and J in §3 stay in play. This is decision 1 in §9.

### A. Cybersecurity, software supply-chain assurance and compliance automation — Tier 1

**Thesis.** Give European software makers and critical-sector buyers the tools that prove what is inside their software, who controls it, and that it meets the Cyber Resilience Act (CRA), NIS2, DORA, GDPR, the AI Act and later the Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA).

**Dependency addressed.** Of 40 Leaders across recent Gartner security Magic Quadrants, 33 were US-based and none EU-based. US and Israeli vendors dominate firewalls, identity and SIEM; European strengths are services (Thales, Orange Cyberdefense) and OT niches **[sourced, Claude]**.

**Demand drivers.**
- CRA is adopted law. Reporting of actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents started 11 Sep 2026, including for products already on the market. The SBOM requirement does not start until 11 Dec 2027, when every product with digital elements also needs vulnerability handling and CE marking. Fines up to €15M or 2.5% of turnover **[sourced, Claude; dates confirmed by web search 13 Oct 2026]**.
- Cybersecurity absorbs about 9% of IT budgets. Compliance is the main investment driver for 70% of organisations. 76% struggle to hire security staff, 71% to retain them **[sourced, GPT, ENISA 2025]**.
- European cybersecurity spend grew 5.2% in 2025; data security +13%, identity +9% **[sourced, Claude]**.
- CADA, if adopted, adds full SBOMs, source-code audits of third-country components and sovereignty risk assessments for public and NIS2 buyers **[sourced, Claude; proposal, not law]**.
- DIGITAL has open calls aimed at helping organisations implement CRA, NIS2, DORA, GDPR and the AI Act **[sourced, GPT]**.

**Where Gemini's proposal lands.** Gemini's #3 is an AI-Act-only compliance engine scored 94%. Claude reports that AI Act high-risk obligations were postponed to 2 Dec 2027 for stand-alone systems and 2 Aug 2028 for embedded ones **[sourced, Claude]**. That postponement removes the near-term buyer deadline Gemini's score depends on. This synthesis treats AI Act compliance as one module inside sector A, not as a sector.

**Moat.** Accumulated telemetry, remediation knowledge, regulatory mappings and CI/CD integrations raise switching costs once embedded (GPT). An EU-owned certification brand that keeps non-EU auditors away from client models (Gemini).

**Business model.** Subscription plus usage-priced API scans plus managed service. Recurring by construction.

**Capital.** Software only. First revenue inside 12–18 months is realistic because the CRA deadline is dated.

**Funding routes.** DIGITAL cybersecurity calls, EIC Transition and Accelerator, security-focused seed funds.

**Entry buyers.** Product manufacturers facing the Dec 2027 CRA deadline; NIS2 operators; public buyers preparing for CADA assurance levels.

**Risks.** Crowded market. Snyk, Anchore, Wiz, CrowdStrike, Microsoft and Palo Alto will add CRA features quickly. Compliance budgets can buy a checkbox instead of a product (Claude). The startup must find a European structural problem where US tools are not merely good enough (GPT).

### B. Grid and energy intelligence — Tier 1

**Thesis.** Software that extracts more capacity, flexibility and reliability from Europe's existing electricity system, which is cheaper and faster than building cables and substations.

**Dependency addressed.** Europe needs an estimated €584B of grid investment by 2030; about 40% of distribution grids are over 40 years old; roughly €730B distribution and €477B transmission investment may be needed by 2040 **[sourced, GPT, Commission]**. Gemini adds foreign-cloud dominance in utility control **[unsourced]**.

**Demand drivers.**
- The June 2026 Strategic Roadmap for Digitalisation and AI in Energy calls for European sovereign AI for grid optimisation, efficiency and demand-side flexibility, and launched the AI.grids initiative **[corroborated, GPT and Claude]**.
- Data-centre sub-case: CADA targets tripling EU data-centre capacity in 5–7 years; an EU data-centre sustainability rating issues first labels in 2027; Germany requires new sites to reuse 10% of waste heat rising to 20% by end 2028; only 1.9% of EU data-centre heat is reused; grid connections in Frankfurt, London, Amsterdam, Paris and Dublin take 7–10 years **[sourced, Claude]**.
- A GridTech venture ecosystem already exists in Europe covering flexibility, battery optimisation, forecasting and network analytics **[sourced, GPT, Dealroom]**.

**Moat.** Proprietary time-series and operational data; grid and market integrations; models that improve with every deployment.

**Business model.** SaaS, revenue share on achieved savings or avoided imbalance penalties, licences to utilities, recurring contracts with industrial loads.

**Capital.** Low for software. Heat reuse and cooling are hardware and fail requirement 4 (Claude).

**Funding routes.** Horizon Europe Cluster 5, DIGITAL, energy VCs such as SET Ventures and World Fund **[unsourced, Gemini]**.

**Where the documents disagree on entry.** Gemini starts with municipal utilities and TSOs through SCADA APIs. GPT warns that TSO and DSO procurement is extremely slow and recommends starting with industrial customers, aggregators and distributed assets. Claude starts with data-centre operators, a concentrated buyer set with long cycles. This synthesis follows GPT: start with asset owners and aggregators, treat data centres as one large-load segment, and move into grid operators later.

**Risks.** Procurement cycles at grid operators. Claude's confidence here is the lowest of its five (65) because the drivers are strategies and delegated acts rather than adopted regulation.

### C. Sovereign cloud and AI software layer — Tier 1

**Thesis.** Do not build data centres. Build the orchestration, platform and migration layer that makes fragmented European infrastructure (OVHcloud, Scaleway, IONOS, STACKIT, T-Systems, national clouds, EuroHPC AI Factories) usable the way a hyperscaler is.

**Dependency addressed.** Three US hyperscalers hold about 70% of the European cloud market. European providers hold about 15% of a €61B market (2024), down from 29% in 2017; SAP and Deutsche Telekom lead at 2% each **[corroborated, GPT and Claude, Synergy Research]**. The EU relies on non-EU providers for more than 80% of digital products, services, infrastructure and IP **[corroborated, GPT and Claude]**.

**Demand drivers.**
- Gartner forecasts European sovereign cloud IaaS spend of $6.9B in 2025, $12.6B in 2026 and $23.1B in 2027, overtaking North America, with governments the main buyers **[sourced, Claude]**.
- CADA proposes four cloud sovereignty assurance levels. Levels 3–4 require EU ownership and control, full SBOMs and source-code audits. "Union added value" becomes a non-price award criterion worth up to about 15 of 120 points **[sourced, Claude; Gemini's "Level 3/4" and GPT's "four assurance levels" agree; proposal, not law]**.
- The Commission explored moving its own hosting from Azure to OVHcloud **[sourced, Claude]**.
- 19 AI Factories and 13 antennas are being established under EuroHPC **[sourced, GPT]**.

**Where a startup fits.** Managed databases, Kubernetes and AI serving that run on several EU clouds; migration and re-platforming tooling; workload placement by cost, latency, sovereignty and regulation; federated inference; model routing; confidential computing; sovereignty-assessment tooling for CADA levels; sovereign-ready packaging for ISVs.

**Positioning.** GPT's framing sells even to customers who stay on AWS or Azure: run each workload where cost, latency, sovereignty and regulation make most sense, without lock-in to one hyperscaler. "Avoid US clouds" is commercially weak.

**Moat.** Cloud integrations, policy engines, European certification, switching infrastructure and an ecosystem around open-source components.

**Capital.** Low to medium. Software only.

**Funding routes.** DIGITAL, EuroHPC and AI Factories, EIC Accelerator.

**Risks.** Hyperscaler sovereign offers (Bleu, S3ns, AWS European Sovereign Cloud) may satisfy Levels 1–2, which cover most buyers. EU providers may build the platform layer themselves. CADA adoption is unlikely before end 2027, so early revenue must come from buyers acting ahead of the law (Claude).

### D. Industrial AI and robotics intelligence — Tier 2

**Thesis.** Build the intelligence layer above Europe's installed industrial base: machines, robots, sensors and operational data. Europe owns the physical layer; EProject helps it own the intelligence layer.

**Dependency addressed.** In 2025 only 17.3% of EU manufacturing companies used AI, against 62.5% in information and communication. About 55% of large businesses use AI versus 17–19% of small ones **[sourced, GPT, Eurostat]**. The EIB names manufacturing as the area where Europe can exploit its industrial base through process optimisation, quality control and downtime reduction **[sourced, GPT]**.

**Demand drivers.** The Apply AI Strategy names manufacturing, engineering, construction and robotics as strategic sectors. Horizon Europe Cluster 4 funds AI, robotics and strategic digital autonomy. AI Factories give SMEs compute and expertise **[sourced, GPT]**.

**Candidate wedges, not products.** Robot programming and adaptation, visual quality inspection, predictive process control, industrial copilots, machine optimisation, digital twins, anomaly detection, human-machine-robot coordination.

**Moat.** Proprietary process data, machine integrations, plant-specific knowledge, operational feedback. Once embedded in production, switching costs are high.

**Business model.** Recurring enterprise software plus deployment and usage fees, with outcome-based pricing in some verticals.

**Capital.** Low. Software on existing machines.

**Funding routes.** Horizon Europe Cluster 4, EIC Accelerator, AI Factories.

**Why Tier 2 and not Tier 1.** GPT ranks it first and its evidence is strong. The other two documents neither proposed nor rejected it, so there is no cross-check. The buyer base is SME-heavy and slow to adopt, which is both the opportunity and the risk **[reviewer]**.

**Risks.** Industrial customers are heterogeneous. The startup can slide into consulting and integration. It must find a repeatable problem (GPT).

**Adjacent.** Gemini's supply-chain and critical-inputs intelligence (E) targets the same manufacturers from the procurement side.

### G. Open-source digital workplace and public-sector software — Tier 2

**Thesis.** European governments have started leaving Microsoft 365 for open-source suites. The ecosystem to migrate, extend, secure and run those suites at scale is being built now.

**Dependency addressed.** The EU spends about €264B a year mainly on US proprietary IT **[sourced, Claude]**.

**Demand drivers.**
- The Open Source Strategy and CADA introduce "open source first" and "public money, public code" for public software purchases **[sourced, Claude; strategy, no legal force on its own]**.
- Schleswig-Holstein moved about 80% of workstations to openDesk and reports over €15M in licence savings. Switzerland is piloting openDesk on 3,000 workstations. Denmark's digital ministry, Copenhagen and Aarhus are phasing out Microsoft **[sourced, Claude]**.
- Since 23 Sep 2026 accredited private providers can sell openDesk Enterprise, and ZenDiS is recruiting operations, sales and implementation partners **[sourced, Claude; unchecked]**.

**Where a startup fits.** Migration tooling from Microsoft 365; enterprise editions for the private sector; admin, security and AI add-ons to openDesk, La Suite and Nextcloud; vertical public-sector applications released as open source.

**Business model.** Product layer (add-ons, migration tooling) plus services. Margins stay in services unless the startup owns a product layer (Claude).

**Capital.** Low. Software and services.

**Funding routes.** DIGITAL, national digital-sovereignty programmes, EIC.

**Manifest fit note.** The manifest calls EProject an AI project. The AI add-on layer (assistants, search, document intelligence inside the sovereign suite) is the slice that satisfies that requirement. Pure migration services do not **[reviewer]**.

**Risks.** Public procurement is slow. The core products are owned by state bodies and foundations.

### E. Supply-chain and critical-inputs intelligence — not explored; wedge candidate

Gemini #2, scored 91/92 with no citations. Thesis: trace and forecast critical raw materials and components across European manufacturing with graph models, and automate Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) and Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD) reporting.

Review: CBAM's definitive period started 1 Jan 2026 and creates real reporting demand for large importers **[reviewer]**. CSDDD was delayed and its scope cut sharply by the 2025 omnibus process, so the regulatory moat Gemini describes is weaker than written **[reviewer, to mid-2026]**. The network-effect moat needs many suppliers on the platform before it works, which is a cold-start problem Gemini does not address. Recommendation: carry it as a wedge candidate inside D (industrial) or A (compliance), not as its own sector.

### F. Vertical sovereign AI for regulated sectors — not explored; delivery pattern

Claude #5, scored 75/60. Thesis: domain AI systems for public administration, energy, health and defence, run on EU compute with AI Act compliance built in.

Review: Claude's own evidence says the model layer is taken (Mistral raised €3B at over €21B in Sep 2026; Aleph Alpha is merging into Cohere **[sourced, Claude; unchecked, post mid-2026]**) and domain applications are fragmented. GPT's "common pattern" describes the same thing: take existing European and open models and embed them in factories, grids, security systems and cloud infrastructure. That is a delivery pattern for sectors A–D rather than a sector. Recommendation: fold it into each exploration file as the delivery pattern, not a separate file.

### H. AI-native semiconductor design stack — dropped (decision 2)

GPT #5 (8.1/8.0) proposes it. Claude lists it among sectors set aside.

GPT's case: Chips Act 2.0 makes design capability a central objective, expands a European virtual chip-design platform with tools, IP and open-source EDA, and the Commission estimates €3–4B could be mobilised for fabless design companies **[sourced, GPT]**. Entry costs €1–3M rather than billions. Tooling embedded in engineering workflows has the deepest moat of the five.

Claude's case against: tape-outs cost tens of millions and take years; Axelera AI needed over €200M **[sourced, Claude]**. Revisit only if the team has silicon experience.

Reconciliation: the two documents describe different companies. GPT's is an EDA and verification software vendor, which never pays for a tape-out. Claude's set-aside is aimed at chip companies. The software version passes requirement 4. What it cannot pass without the right people is the team test: integrating into conservative design flows needs founders with semiconductor and ML depth. Decision 2 in §9.

### I. Precision agro-ecology and bio-input optimisation — set aside

Gemini #4 (85/83), unsourced. Thesis: Copernicus satellite data plus low-cost sensors to time biological inputs and cut imported synthetic fertiliser, distributed through farming cooperatives. Funding: Horizon Europe Cluster 6, EIT Food, Astanor Ventures, Ananda Impact Ventures.

Review: fertiliser import dependence is a real European vulnerability **[reviewer]**, and the software-only entry fits requirement 4. It fails only the digital reading of "tech sovereignty". Out by decision 1.

### J. Dual-use autonomous swarm logistics — set aside

Gemini #5 (82/80), unsourced. Thesis: hardware-agnostic orchestration software for unmanned aerial and ground vehicle swarms, developed in simulation first. Funding: European Defence Fund and the EU Defence Innovation Scheme (Gemini writes "EIDIS"; the scheme is EUDIS **[reviewer]**).

Review: simulation-first entry is low capital, but revenue depends on defence procurement cycles, export controls and security clearance, and many European VCs exclude defence **[reviewer]**. It fails requirement 3 on the VC side and the digital reading of the mission. Out by decision 1.

## 6. The common pattern

All three documents arrive at the same shape from different directions.

- GPT: **AI × strategically important European infrastructure × proprietary operational data × difficult integration × European regulation and standards.** Avoid businesses whose moat is a prompt, an interface or a short-lived model advantage.
- Claude: the openings are in the **software layers on top of sovereign infrastructure**, which can serve compliance deadlines and procurement preferences within 12–24 months. Fabs, frontier models and hyperscale clouds need billions and decades.
- Gemini: embed AI in **heavy industry, supply chains, regulatory frameworks and critical infrastructure**, not lightweight applications.

Consequence for every exploration file: the candidate must name the infrastructure it sits on, the operational data it accumulates, the integration that makes it hard to replace, and the European rule or standard that makes a European vendor preferable.

## 10. Exploration template for each selected sector

Each `02.x-<sector>.md` answers the following, in this order. Items 1 and 2 are GPT's method; items 3–8 come from Claude's product-phase questions and the manifest.

1. **Decompose** the sector into 5–10 concrete structural problems. Score problems, not products.
2. **For each problem**, establish: who pays for it today and how much; why existing solutions fail; what AI changed in the economics recently; what proprietary asset EProject would hold after five years that a new entrant cannot reproduce.
3. **First buyer.** Public administration, NIS2 operator, software manufacturer, industrial plant, aggregator. One segment, named.
4. **First countries.** Germany and France lead on sovereignty procurement; Denmark and Switzerland are early movers (Claude). Confirm for the sector.
5. **Regulatory clock.** Which obligation, which date, adopted or proposal (use §7).
6. **Competitive response.** Which US incumbents will add the feature, which EU incumbents exist, and the "good enough" test from GPT.
7. **Funding.** Which EU instrument and call, which VC thesis, and the stage at which the Scaleup Europe Fund becomes relevant. If CADA matters, which assurance level the product targets.
8. **Manifest check.** Requirements 1–4 explicitly, with the capital test applied to every hardware-adjacent element.

Expected output per sector: a shortlist of two or three structural problems ranked for the product phase, with status tags on every figure.

**Problem scoring method.** Each problem gets a score from 1 to 5 on six factors, for a maximum of 30:

- **Pain:** a budget exists and an owner feels the problem.
- **Clock:** a dated trigger forces action.
- **AI lift:** AI changes the economics now.
- **Moat:** a proprietary asset accumulates with use.
- **Capital:** software only, with revenue inside 18 months.
- **Gap:** no dominant vendor that European buyers prefer.

The scores are the reviewer's judgement. Treat differences of 2 points or less as a tie.
