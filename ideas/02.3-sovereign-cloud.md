## Bottom line

Sovereign cloud spending is real and confirmed. Gartner forecasts it to triple in Europe between 2025 and 2027. The platform layer above European clouds is filling faster than the baseline assumed, though. A consortium of OVHcloud, Clever Cloud and DEEP won the Commission's €180M sovereign cloud tender, with Clever Cloud supplying the platform layer. The best open entries are therefore **AI-assisted migration off hyperscalers** and **sovereign-deployment packaging for software vendors**. **Workload sovereignty assessment** is the bridge to CADA. A generic multi-cloud platform is no longer a gap.

## 2. The shortlisted problems in detail

### C1 — AI-assisted migration

- **Who pays today.** Gartner expects about 20% of new sovereign cloud spending to come from moving existing workloads to local providers **[web]**. Today, integrators do this by hand.
- **Why existing solutions fail.** Hyperscaler migration tools only move workloads into their own clouds. EU providers do not offer equivalent tools for moving workloads out **[assumed]**.
- **What AI changed.** Language models can translate CloudFormation, Terraform and application code that depends on proprietary services into portable equivalents. That cuts the integrator's hours sharply **[assumed]**.
- **The asset after five years.** A library of service mappings and translations between each hyperscaler and each EU provider, validated by real migrations.
- **Market size.** Gartner forecasts European sovereign cloud IaaS spending of $6.9B in 2025, $12.6B in 2026 and $23.1B in 2027. Governments are the main buyers, followed by regulated industries and critical-infrastructure operators **[web, Gartner primary]**.

### C7 — Sovereign-deployment packaging for software vendors

- **Who pays today.** European software vendors lose public and critical-sector deals when they can only run on US clouds **[assumed]**.
- **Why it fits.** A product that lets a SaaS vendor deploy into the customer's own EU cloud sells to vendors in every sector. It reuses C1's service mappings.
- **The asset after five years.** A catalogue of vendors packaged for each EU provider, with the assurance level each deployment achieves.

### C2 — Workload sovereignty assessment

- **Who pays today.** Under the CADA proposal, Level 1 needs EU establishment, EU data residency including metadata and telemetry, and transparent subcontracting. Middle levels add independent verification and SBOMs. Higher levels restrict ownership, control and exposure to third-country law. One reading says Level 3 also requires EU-citizen operating staff **[web]**. Buyers will need to know which workloads need which level.
- **Overlap.** C2 is the buyer-side view of A5 in sector A (`02.1`). A5 assesses suppliers, and C2 assesses the buyer's own workloads. Build one of the two, not both.
- **The risk.** It depends on CADA, which is a proposal **[sourced]**.

## 3. Entry decisions

- **First buyer.** Regulated mid-size firms and public bodies with a sovereignty mandate who are already moving workloads. Then European software vendors who sell to them, through C7.
- **First countries.** France, where SecNumCloud and the €180M Commission contract won by OVHcloud, Clever Cloud and DEEP set the pattern **[web]**. Germany comes next.
- **Regulatory clock.** CADA was proposed on 3 Jun 2026 and adoption is unlikely before end 2027 **[web, sourced]**. Demand before then comes from buyers acting on geopolitics.
- **Competitive response.** Hyperscaler sovereign offerings, such as Bleu, S3ns and the AWS European Sovereign Cloud, can meet the lower levels **[sourced]**. EU providers are building their own platform layers: Clever Cloud launched its own Kubernetes engine on 27 Apr 2026 **[web]**. Migration tooling still has no native European owner **[assumed]**.
- **Funding.** DIGITAL, the EIC Accelerator, and the AI Factories for inference work. The Synergy figures still stand: EU providers hold about 15% of the market and the three US hyperscalers about 70% **[web]**.
- **Manifest check.** All four requirements pass. The product is European, it directly reduces cloud dependence, and it has funding routes. It is software only with a moat in service mappings. C3 and C5 fail the capital and gap tests, so they are excluded.

## 4. Shortlist for the product phase

1. **C1:** AI-assisted migration from hyperscalers to EU clouds.
2. **C7:** sovereign-deployment packaging for software vendors, built on C1's mappings.
3. **C2:** workload sovereignty assessment, merged with A5 in sector A.
