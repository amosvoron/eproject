## Bottom line

The adoption gap is confirmed, though one figure changes. Manufacturing AI adoption is about 17%, against about 20% across all EU sectors in 2025. Sources disagree whether the 17.3% figure is for 2024 or 2025. Robotics capital in Europe is concentrated on hardware: European robotics companies raised €1.8B in H1 2026, and Neura alone raised €1.2B. The software openings are therefore in **service intelligence for machine builders**, **capture of industrial know-how**, and **process-parameter optimisation**. Visual inspection and predictive maintenance are crowded. The Data Act and the Machinery Regulation give dated support to the first two openings.

## 2. The shortlisted problems in detail

### D3 — Service intelligence for machine builders

- **Who pays today.** Machine builders earn high-margin service revenue but lack trained technicians. Their knowledge is spread across manuals, tickets and the heads of senior engineers **[reviewer]**.
- **Why existing solutions fail.** Field-service software manages tickets and scheduling. It does not diagnose a fault from the machine's history and documentation **[assumed]**.
- **What AI changed.** Language models over manuals, service logs and sensor data can now draft a diagnosis and a repair procedure. Generic copilots cannot do this without the builder's private data **[assumed]**.
- **The asset after five years.** A failure-to-fix knowledge graph per machine family, which gets better with every service call. It is the strongest moat in this sector.
- **Synergy.** The same machine builders face the CRA (`02.1`, A2 and A9). A vendor that already holds their product data can sell both products **[assumed]**.

### D6 — Capturing industrial know-how

- **Who pays today.** Plants lose process knowledge as experienced staff retire. They pay for it in scrap, downtime and training time **[reviewer]**.
- **What AI changed.** Speech and language models can now interview experts, watch work done on the line, and turn both into structured instructions **[assumed]**.
- **Overlap.** D6 feeds D3. Captured knowledge becomes the machine builder's or the plant's diagnostic base.

### D4 — Process-parameter optimisation

- **Who pays today.** Process engineers tune parameters by experience. Scrap and energy costs follow directly from the settings they choose **[reviewer]**.
- **The asset after five years.** Process models per material and machine type. It is a strong moat, but each deployment needs integration, which risks turning the company into a consultancy **[sourced, GPT]**.
- **Link to sector B.** Parameter optimisation can include energy, which connects D4 to B5 in `02.2`.

## 3. Entry decisions

- **First buyer.** Mid-size machine builders, the German *Mittelstand* type, who sell machines with service contracts. One builder gives access to many installed machines.
- **First countries.** Germany, Austria and northern Italy, which hold Europe's largest concentration of machine builders **[reviewer]**.
- **Regulatory clock.** The Data Act has applied since 12 Sep 2025. It gives machine users rights to their machine data and opens that data to third-party services **[reviewer]**. The Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies from 20 Jan 2027 and permits digital instructions **[reviewer]**. The CRA covers machines with digital elements from Dec 2027 **[web]**.
- **Competitive response.** Large automation vendors such as Siemens sell industrial copilots **[reviewer]**. Robotics startups such as Wandelbots, RobCo and Micropsi address D2 **[reviewer]**. D3 sold to builders that are not Siemens customers is less contested **[assumed]**.
- **Funding.** The Apply AI Strategy covers manufacturing and robotics, with flagship actions announced on 8 Oct and about €120M expected in 2026 Horizon Europe calls for collaborative AI in manufacturing and robotics **[web, conference summary; verify]**. Other routes are the EIC Accelerator and the AI Factories. Robotics venture money is strong but goes mainly to hardware **[web]**.
- **Manifest check.** All four requirements pass. The product is European and builds on Europe's industrial base. It has EU and VC funding routes. It is software on existing machines, with a moat in proprietary process data.

## 4. Shortlist for the product phase

1. **D3:** service intelligence for machine builders.
2. **D6:** know-how capture, as the first module feeding D3.
3. **D4:** process-parameter optimisation, kept as the expansion into plant operations.
