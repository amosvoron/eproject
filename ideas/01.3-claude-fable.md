# European Tech Sovereignty: 5 Startup Opportunity Sectors

Oct 9, 2026 · @Amos Voron

## Summary

The strongest startup openings are in the software layers that sit on top of Europe's sovereign infrastructure, not in the infrastructure itself. The June 2026 package creates compliance deadlines and procurement preferences that software companies can serve within 12–24 months, while fabs, frontier models and hyperscale clouds need billions and decade-long horizons.

1. **Sovereign cybersecurity and software supply-chain assurance**: proving what is inside software and who controls it, for CRA, NIS2 and CADA.
2. **Sovereign cloud platform and migration layer**: making EU clouds as easy to adopt as the hyperscalers, and moving workloads onto them.
3. **Open-source digital workplace and public-sector software**: deploying, extending and running the European Microsoft 365 alternatives now being adopted.
4. **Data-centre energy and grid integration**: efficiency, heat reuse, flexibility and reporting for a capacity build-out that must triple under tightening rules.
5. **Vertical sovereign AI for regulated and critical sectors**: domain AI systems for public administration, energy, health and defence, run on EU compute.

The top three sectors are close enough to be treated as a group; the bottom two carry more capital need or more competition.

## Context: why now

The 3 June 2026 package turns sovereignty from a political slogan into procurement rules, permitting fast tracks and certification levels, which creates buyers with deadlines. The EU is reliant on non-EU providers for more than 80% of its digital products, services, infrastructure and IP, and spends about €264 billion a year mainly on US proprietary IT ([Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/eus-technological-sovereignty-package-sets-new-direction-digital-economy)).

| Package element | Type | What it changes for a startup |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA)](https://www.insideglobaltech.com/2026/06/11/the-eu-cloud-and-ai-development-act-in-depth/) | Regulation (proposal, in trilogue) | Four cloud sovereignty assurance levels for public-sector and NIS2-sector buyers; Level 3–4 require EU ownership and control, full SBOMs, source-code audits. "Union added value" becomes a non-price award criterion (up to \~15 of 120 points). Target: triple EU data-centre capacity in 5–7 years; 12-month permits in acceleration zones. |
| [Chips Act 2.0](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/06/eu-tech-sovereignty-package/) | Regulation (proposal) | Demand Accelerators and public innovation procurement for EU-designed chips; a Chips Fund for start-ups and SMEs; first advanced EU fab pilot production 2030–2033. |
| [Open Source Strategy](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/06/eu-tech-sovereignty-package/) | Strategy | "Open source first" for public cloud and AI software purchases, "public money, public code", an EU Open Source Solutions Catalogue and OSPOs across administrations. |
| [Digitalisation and AI in Energy roadmap](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/06/eu-tech-sovereignty-package/) | Strategy + delegated act | EU data-centre sustainability rating (adopted 2026, first labels 2027), minimum performance standards under consultation, and sovereign AI models for the energy sector. |

The market signal is already visible. European providers hold only about 15% of a €61 billion European cloud market, down from 29% in 2017 ([Synergy Research](https://srgresearch.com/articles/european-cloud-providers-local-market-share-now-holds-steady-at-15)), while Gartner expects European sovereign cloud IaaS spend to grow from $6.9 billion in 2025 to $23.1 billion in 2027, overtaking North America ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-02-09-gartner-says-worldwide-sovereign-cloud-iaas-spending-will-total-us-dollars-80-billion-in-2026)).

Funding is lining up behind it: the €5 billion Scaleup Europe Fund, managed by EQT, launched in August 2026 for AI, quantum, bio and clean tech ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/142876-eu-establishes-5b-scaleup-europe-fund-with-eqt-as-manager/)), and the EIC has €1.4 billion for deep-tech start-ups in its 2026 work programme ([European Commission](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/new-eu14-billion-boost-europes-deep-tech-scale-ups-commission-adopts-2026-european-innovation-2025-11-10_en)). The main caveat is timing: CADA and Chips Act 2.0 are unlikely to be adopted before end 2027, so early revenue must come from buyers acting ahead of the law.

## How the scores work

**Viability (0–100)** asks whether a new, venture-backed European startup can build a large business in the sector within about five years. Each sector gets 1–5 on five factors; the weighted average is multiplied by 20.

| Factor | Weight | What a 5 means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand pull and timing | 25% | Buyers face dated obligations or are already switching |
| Market size and growth | 20% | Multi-billion euro spend growing fast |
| Incumbent gap | 20% | No dominant European player and buyers want to avoid the US one |
| Capital efficiency | 20% | Software-led, revenue possible within 12–18 months |
| Policy and funding tailwind | 15% | Named in the package with money or procurement preference behind it |

**Confidence (0–100)** is how much the viability score can be trusted. It rises when the drivers are adopted law rather than proposals, when market numbers come from independent analysts, and when real buyers have already moved. It falls when the market is moving fast or the evidence is mostly forecasts.

| Sector | Demand | Market | Gap | Capital | Policy | Viability | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cybersecurity and supply-chain assurance | 5 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 86 | 75 |
| Sovereign cloud platform and migration | 5 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 82 | 70 |
| Open-source workplace and public software | 4 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 80 | 70 |
| Data-centre energy and grid integration | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 76 | 65 |
| Vertical sovereign AI | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 5 | 75 | 60 |

The scores are a reasoned judgement from the sources below, not a model output; treat differences under 5 points as a tie.

## The five sectors

### 1. Sovereign cybersecurity and software supply-chain assurance

**Viability 86 · Confidence 75.** Every software maker selling in the EU and every critical-sector buyer now has to prove what is in their software and who controls it, and almost no European vendor leads in the tools that do this.

- **Demand drivers.** The Cyber Resilience Act is adopted law: reporting of actively exploited vulnerabilities started on 11 September 2026, and from 11 December 2027 every product with digital elements needs a machine-readable SBOM, vulnerability handling and CE marking, with fines up to €15 million or 2.5% of turnover ([Docker CRA summary](https://www.docker.com/blog/eu-cyber-resilience-act-overview/)). CADA adds full SBOMs, source-code audits of third-country components and sovereignty risk assessments for public and NIS2 buyers ([Inside Global Tech](https://www.insideglobaltech.com/2026/06/11/the-eu-cloud-and-ai-development-act-in-depth/)).
- **Market.** European cybersecurity spend grew 5.2% in 2025, with data security up 13% and identity up 9% ([Context](https://www.contextworld.com/europe-s-cybersecurity-market-finds-growth-beyond-its-traditional-powerhouses)).
- **Incumbent gap.** Of 40 Leaders across recent Gartner security Magic Quadrants, 33 were US-based and none EU-based; US and Israeli vendors dominate firewalls, identity and SIEM ([Cybersec & Biz](https://cybersecandbiz.substack.com/p/where-are-the-european-vendors-in), [European Parliament](<https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/780413/ECTI_ATA(2025)780413_EN.pdf>)). Europe's strengths are services (Thales, Orange Cyberdefense) and OT niches.
- **Where a startup fits.** SBOM and vulnerability-reporting automation for manufacturers, sovereignty and supply-chain risk assessment for public buyers, and EU-controlled versions of core security tools (identity, detection) for Level 3–4 workloads.
- **Main risks.** US tools (Snyk, Anchore, Wiz and others) will add CRA features fast; compliance budgets can be bought as a checkbox rather than a product.

### 2. Sovereign cloud platform and migration layer

**Viability 82 · Confidence 70.** Money for sovereign cloud is arriving faster than European providers can offer a hyperscaler-grade developer experience, and that gap is where startups fit.

- **Demand drivers.** Gartner forecasts European sovereign cloud IaaS spend rising from $6.9 billion in 2025 to $12.6 billion in 2026 and $23.1 billion in 2027, overtaking North America, with governments the main buyers ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-02-09-gartner-says-worldwide-sovereign-cloud-iaas-spending-will-total-us-dollars-80-billion-in-2026)). CADA's assurance levels will push public and critical-sector workloads to EU-controlled providers, and "Union added value" becomes an award criterion.
- **Market.** The European cloud market was €61 billion in 2024 and growing about 24% in 2025; European providers hold about 15%, led by SAP and Deutsche Telekom at 2% each, then OVHcloud, Telecom Italia and Orange ([Synergy Research](https://srgresearch.com/articles/european-cloud-providers-local-market-share-now-holds-steady-at-15)).
- **Incumbent gap.** EU providers such as OVHcloud, Scaleway, IONOS and STACKIT sell solid infrastructure but thin managed services. Even the Commission explored moving its own hosting from Azure to OVHcloud ([Next](https://next.ink/189030/la-commission-europeenne-negocierait-le-passage-de-son-cloud-de-microsoft-a-ovhcloud/)).
- **Where a startup fits.** Managed platforms (databases, Kubernetes, AI serving) that run on several EU clouds, migration and re-platforming tooling, multi-cloud cost and portability control, and sovereign-ready packaging for ISVs.
- **Main risks.** Hyperscaler "sovereign" offers (Bleu, S3ns, AWS European Sovereign Cloud) may satisfy Levels 1–2, which covers most buyers; EU providers may build the platform layer themselves.

### 3. Open-source digital workplace and public-sector software

**Viability 80 · Confidence 70.** European governments have started leaving Microsoft 365 for open-source suites, and the ecosystem to deploy, extend and support them at scale is only now being built.

- **Demand drivers.** The Open Source Strategy and CADA introduce "open source first" and "public money, public code" for public software purchases ([Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/06/eu-tech-sovereignty-package/)). Schleswig-Holstein has moved about 80% of its workstations to openDesk and reports over €15 million in licence savings; Switzerland is piloting it on 3,000 workstations ([Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/tech/opendesk-private-sector/)). Denmark's digital ministry, Copenhagen and Aarhus are phasing out Microsoft ([Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2025/denmark-digital-ministry-drops-microsoft)).
- **Market.** EU firms invested about €1 billion in open source in 2018 for a €65–95 billion economic effect; 10% more contributions would add 0.4–0.6% to GDP ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-study-impact-open-source-european-economy)).
- **Incumbent gap.** Since 23 September 2026, accredited private providers can sell openDesk Enterprise, and ZenDiS is recruiting operations, sales and implementation partners ([Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/tech/opendesk-private-sector/)). Few companies yet combine migration, hosting and add-on product.
- **Where a startup fits.** Migration tooling and services from Microsoft 365, enterprise editions for the private sector, admin, security and AI add-ons to openDesk, La Suite or Nextcloud, and vertical public-sector applications released as open source.
- **Main risks.** Public procurement is slow; core products are owned by state bodies or foundations, so margins sit in services unless the startup owns a product layer.

### 4. Data-centre energy and grid integration

**Viability 76 · Confidence 65.** Europe wants to triple data-centre capacity while grids are full and energy rules tighten, so efficiency, heat reuse and flexibility become a condition of building.

- **Demand drivers.** CADA targets tripling EU data-centre capacity in 5–7 years ([Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/eus-technological-sovereignty-package-sets-new-direction-digital-economy)). An EU sustainability rating covering energy, water, heat reuse and flexibility issues its first labels in 2027 ([Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/06/eu-tech-sovereignty-package/)). Germany requires new sites to reuse 10% of waste heat, rising to 20% by end 2028, and France requires recovery above 1 MW ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/green-data-centers-pioneering-energy-efficiency-and-sustainability-in-the-eu)).
- **Market.** Data centres use 2.5% of EU electricity today; European data-centre demand is projected to rise 150% from 2024 to 2035 ([Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/eus-technological-sovereignty-package-sets-new-direction-digital-economy), [Ember](https://ember-energy.org/?p=12604)).
- **Incumbent gap.** Only 1.9% of EU data-centre heat is reused and 8% of sites recover heat ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/06/green-data-centers-pioneering-energy-efficiency-and-sustainability-in-the-eu)). Grid connections in Frankfurt, London, Amsterdam, Paris and Dublin take 7–10 years on average ([Ember](https://ember-energy.org/?p=12604)).
- **Where a startup fits.** Grid-flexibility and demand-response software for compute loads, heat-reuse project development, energy and water reporting for the rating scheme, siting and connection analytics, and efficient cooling.
- **Main risks.** Hardware-heavy parts need more capital; buyers are a concentrated set of operators with long sales cycles.

### 5. Vertical sovereign AI for regulated and critical sectors

**Viability 75 · Confidence 60.** Public bodies and critical industries want AI they control, and the general-purpose model layer is consolidating, which leaves room for domain systems built on European models and compute.

- **Demand drivers.** CADA's Cloud and AI Leadership Initiatives cover public-sector and industrial AI, and the energy roadmap calls for sovereign AI models for the energy sector ([Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/06/eu-tech-sovereignty-package/)). AI Act high-risk obligations now start on 2 December 2027 for stand-alone systems and 2 August 2028 for embedded ones ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)).
- **Market.** Investors are paying for sovereign AI: Mistral raised €3 billion at over €21 billion in September 2026, co-led by the Scaleup Europe Fund, and expects $1 billion ARR by year-end ([French Tech Journal](https://frenchtechjournal.com/mistral-ai-3-billion-funding-21-billion-valuation)).
- **Incumbent gap.** The model layer is taken: Mistral leads, and Aleph Alpha is merging into Cohere ([Leaders League](https://www.leadersleague.com/en/news/cohere-and-german-ai-company-aleph-alpha-sign-definitive-agreement-to-create-transatlantic-sovereign-ai-group)). Domain applications for grids, hospitals, courts, defence and public services remain fragmented.
- **Where a startup fits.** Sector-specific AI systems with domain data, on-premises or EU-cloud deployment, and AI Act compliance built in.
- **Main risks.** The fastest-moving and most crowded area; model providers and hyperscalers may move down into verticals, and compute and data costs are high.

## Sectors considered and set aside

| Sector | Why it is not in the five |
| --- | --- |
| Advanced semiconductor manufacturing | The EU makes about 10% of global chips and its first advanced fab pilot is planned for 2030–2033 ([Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/eus-technological-sovereignty-package-sets-new-direction-digital-economy), [Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/06/eu-tech-sovereignty-package/)); this is a state and industrial-group play, not a startup one. |
| AI and edge chip design | Real demand and a new Chips Fund, and Axelera AI raised over €200 million with EIC backing ([EIC](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/news/congratulations-axelera-ai-securing-more-eu200-million-funding-global-commercial-growth-2026-02-24_en)). But tape-outs cost tens of millions and take years; worth revisiting if the team has silicon experience. |
| Frontier foundation models | Mistral and Cohere with Aleph Alpha already hold the sovereign model position with billions in funding. |
| New hyperscale cloud provider | European providers have held only 15% share since 2022 despite growth; another infrastructure player needs capital on the scale of OVHcloud or Schwarz Digits. |

## Questions for the product phase

- [ ] Which buyer to start with in each sector: public administrations, NIS2 critical operators, or software manufacturers?
- [ ] Which CADA assurance level each product targets, since Levels 3–4 exclude hyperscalers but cover fewer buyers.
- [ ] Which countries first: Germany and France lead on sovereignty procurement, with Denmark and Switzerland as early movers.
- [ ] How to fund it: EIC Accelerator, Chips Fund, national programmes or private VC, and at what stage the Scaleup Europe Fund becomes relevant.
- [ ] How to track CADA and Chips Act 2.0 through trilogue, since adoption before end 2027 is unlikely.
