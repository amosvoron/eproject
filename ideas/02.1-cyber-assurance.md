## Bottom line

Sector A is the strongest of the five on timing, because it has adopted law with dated obligations. The best entry is **CRA conformity automation for mid-size makers of machines and devices**, with **firmware and binary SBOM analysis** as the technical depth that general SBOM tools lack. A **sovereignty risk-assessment tool for public buyers** stays as an option and depends on CADA passing. The main risk is crowding. US supply-chain tools and at least one funded European startup already target the CRA.

## 2. The shortlisted problems in detail

### A2 — CRA conformity automation

- **Who pays today.** Manufacturers of products with digital elements pay consultancies and notified bodies, or spend internal engineering time. From 11 Dec 2027 they need a machine-readable SBOM, vulnerability handling, a defined support period and CE marking. Fines reach €15M or 2.5% of turnover **[web]**.
- **Why existing solutions fail.** US SBOM tools such as Anchore and Snyk produce the SBOM. They do not produce the rest of the technical file, which includes the risk assessment, the support-period justification and the declaration of conformity **[assumed]**. Consultancies do produce it, but by hand and per product.
- **What AI changed.** Language models can now draft risk assessments and technical documentation from code, architecture docs and SBOMs. That turns a consulting deliverable into a software product **[assumed]**.
- **The asset after five years.** A mapping from product types to CRA requirements to accepted evidence, refined across hundreds of conformity files and notified-body reviews.
- **Who already competes.** CRACI, a Helsinki startup, raised €1.4M pre-seed in May 2026 for CRA compliance built into CI/CD pipelines **[web]**. Compliance platforms such as Vanta and Drata can add CRA templates **[assumed]**.

### A9 — Firmware and binary SBOM analysis

- **Who pays today.** Makers of machines, devices and industrial controllers often do not hold source code for their supplier components. They still need an SBOM and vulnerability monitoring for the whole product **[reviewer]**.
- **Why existing solutions fail.** Source-based SBOM tools cannot see inside a binary blob from a supplier. Binary analysis is a specialist niche **[reviewer]**.
- **What AI changed.** Models can now identify components and versions in stripped binaries, and they can rank which vulnerabilities are reachable. Both used to take expert manual work **[assumed]**.
- **The asset after five years.** A fingerprint library of firmware components found in European industrial products, together with exploitability data.
- **Who already competes.** ONEKEY in Germany works on firmware analysis. Finite State and NetRise in the US do too **[reviewer]**. This is a real gap, but not an empty one.

### A5 — Supplier sovereignty assessment

- **Who pays today.** Under the CADA proposal, Member States would run sovereignty risk assessments. Contracting authorities whose cloud use is relevant to public order would then have to buy at a higher assurance level **[web]**. Today, buyers do this by hand or rely on France's SecNumCloud **[web]**.
- **Why it is only an option.** CADA is a proposal. Adoption is unlikely before end 2027 **[sourced]**. Demand before then comes only from buyers who act ahead of the law.
- **The asset after five years.** A register of providers, subcontractors and ownership chains, mapped to assurance levels. Every buyer would reuse it.
- **Who already competes.** No dedicated vendor was found **[assumed]**. This is the clearest gap in the sector.

## 3. Entry decisions

- **First buyer.** Mid-size makers of machines, devices and industrial controllers, with roughly 50–1,000 employees. They are inside CRA scope, they lack in-house compliance teams, and they often ship supplier firmware. Pure SaaS vendors are mostly outside the CRA, so they are the wrong first buyer **[reviewer]**.
- **First countries.** Germany first, because of its machine and device makers. Then Austria, the Netherlands and the Nordics **[assumed]**.
- **Regulatory clock.** CRA reporting has applied since 11 Sep 2026 and covers products already on the market. The full CRA obligations apply from 11 Dec 2027. Commission guidance with 67 worked examples came out on 27 Jul 2026 **[web]**. The CADA date is open.
- **Competitive response.** US SBOM vendors will add CRA document generation. The defence is depth in firmware and the product's OT context, plus a German-language, notified-body-ready output **[assumed]**.
- **Funding.** DIGITAL cybersecurity calls run through the European Cybersecurity Competence Centre **[reviewer]**. The EIC Accelerator and security seed funds are the other routes. CRACI's round shows that Nordic seed money exists for this thesis **[web]**.
- **Manifest check.** All four requirements pass. The product is European and reduces dependence on US security tooling. It has EU and VC funding routes. It is software only, with a moat in compliance mappings and a firmware library.

## 4. Shortlist for the product phase

1. **A2 + A9 combined:** a CRA conformity product for machine and device makers, with firmware SBOM as the differentiator.
2. **A5:** sovereignty assessment for public buyers, kept as a second product once CADA progresses.

The same buyers, makers of machines and devices, also appear in sector D (`02.4`, problem D3). §11 of `02.0` covers that overlap.
