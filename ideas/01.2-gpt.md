Looking at the European market through the specific lens you defined for **EProject**, I would not start from “where can AI make money?” I would start from:

The 2026 EIC work programme alone allocates more than **€1.4 billion**, including €634 million for the EIC Accelerator, while DIGITAL, Horizon Europe, Chips JU, EuroHPC/AI Factories and STEP cover many of the sectors below. [European Innovation Council](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/eic-funding-opportunities/eic-2026-work-programme_en?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 1. European Industrial AI & Robotics Intelligence

**Thesis:** build the intelligence/software layer that makes European factories, industrial machinery and robots substantially more autonomous, productive and adaptable.

This is the opportunity I would investigate first.

Europe's comparative advantage is not likely to be another frontier LLM competing head-on with US companies. Europe has something much harder to reproduce: **a huge industrial installed base, sophisticated manufacturing knowledge, machinery, automation companies, automotive suppliers and robotics expertise**.

Yet AI penetration into that industrial base remains surprisingly low. In 2025 only **17.3% of EU manufacturing companies used AI**, compared with 62.5% in information/communications. And AI adoption remains heavily skewed by company size: around 55% of large European businesses use AI versus only 17–19% of small businesses depending on the Eurostat measure. [European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/7870049/23260410/KS-01-26-009-EN-N.pdf/37d063cb-28cf-3b4e-91f3-c3784c970842?t=\&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The EIB's 2025/26 analysis is particularly important: it finds that AI adoption is associated with higher productivity and explicitly identifies manufacturing as an area where Europe can exploit its industrial base for process optimisation, quality control, downtime reduction and supply-chain improvement. [European Investment Bank](https://www.eib.org/files/publications/20250379-030326-investment-report-2025-chapter5-en.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

So the sector is broader than “robotics startup.” The valuable layer lies between existing industrial assets and AI:

**machines + robots + sensors + operational data → European industrial intelligence**

Possible investigation areas for the next phase would include robot programming and adaptation, visual quality inspection, predictive process control, industrial copilots, machine optimisation, digital twins, anomaly detection and coordination between humans, machines and robots. Those are **wedge examples, not yet product choices**.

The moat can become excellent because industrial AI accumulates things generic AI companies do not have: proprietary process data, machine integrations, domain models, plant-specific knowledge and operational feedback. Once software becomes embedded in production, switching costs can also become substantial.

A strong business model would likely be recurring enterprise software plus usage/deployment fees, eventually with outcome-based pricing in certain verticals.

The EU funding fit is exceptionally strong. The Apply AI Strategy explicitly identifies **manufacturing, engineering, construction and robotics** as strategic sectors. Horizon Europe's Digital, Industry and Space programme is funding AI, robotics and strategic digital autonomy, and EuroHPC's AI Factories are designed to make compute and expertise available to European SMEs and industrial users. There are now 19 AI Factories and 13 associated antennas being established. [Digital Strategy](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/factpages/apply-ai-strategy-overview?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Why it fits EProject especially well:** Europe already owns much of the physical industrial layer. EProject would help Europe own more of the intelligence layer sitting above it.

**Main risk:** industrial customers are heterogeneous. The eventual startup must resist becoming a consulting/integration company and discover a sufficiently repeatable problem.

**Viability: 9.2/10**  
**Confidence: 9.1/10**

## 2. European AI Cyber-Resilience Stack

**Thesis:** build AI-native European cybersecurity capabilities for European companies, critical infrastructure and software supply chains.

Cybersecurity has one of the strongest combinations of **immediate willingness to pay + recurring revenue + strategic autonomy + regulation-driven demand + low startup capital** of all the sectors examined.

And there is a structural labour shortage which AI can directly attack.

ENISA's 2025 research finds cybersecurity still absorbs about **9% of organisations' IT budgets**. Compliance is now the main driver of cybersecurity investment for 70% of organisations. Meanwhile, 76% report difficulty attracting cybersecurity professionals and 71% difficulty retaining them. Patching, business continuity and supply-chain risk management remain particularly difficult. [ENISA](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/news/whats-driving-cybersecurity-investments-and-where-lie-the-challenges?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Europe has simultaneously created a regulatory demand engine through **NIS2, the Cyber Resilience Act, DORA, GDPR and the AI Act**.

This is unusually attractive because regulation isn't simply a constraint here. It creates a home-field advantage.

A European cybersecurity company can encode European regulatory requirements, European infrastructure assumptions, data-sovereignty requirements and European threat intelligence into its technology. That creates differentiation against generic American security tooling.

The sector-level opportunity is therefore not “an AI cybersecurity chatbot.” It is closer to:

> **European autonomous cyber defence and resilience infrastructure.**

The eventual investigation could explore vulnerability management, automated remediation, software-supply-chain security, continuous NIS2/CRA assurance, AI-agent security, incident analysis, SME security automation or protection of operational technology.

Again, those are candidate wedges rather than a predetermined product.

The economics are attractive: subscription software, managed services and enterprise contracts all provide recurring revenue. Capital requirements can remain relatively low because EProject would build software rather than security hardware.

EU funding alignment is almost perfect. DIGITAL currently has calls specifically aimed at strengthening European cybersecurity capabilities and helping organisations implement CRA, NIS2, DORA, GDPR and AI Act requirements. [Digital Strategy](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/activities/get-funding-digital?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**The moat:** accumulated security telemetry, automated remediation knowledge, proprietary threat models, regulatory mappings, integrations and trust.

**Main risk:** cybersecurity is crowded. EProject would have to find a European structural problem where CrowdStrike/Microsoft/Palo Alto/etc. are not merely good enough.

That is why I rank it fractionally below industrial AI despite its excellent economics.

**Viability: 9.1/10**  
**Confidence: 9.3/10**

## 3. Sovereign European AI & Cloud Software Layer

**Thesis:** do not try to build another AWS. Build the software layer that makes fragmented European compute, cloud and AI infrastructure behave like a coherent European platform.

This is probably the project that **most literally embodies EProject's mission**.

Three US hyperscalers—AWS, Microsoft and Google—control around **70% of the European cloud infrastructure market**. European providers collectively have around 15%, despite the European market reaching roughly €61 billion in 2024. [Synergy Research Group](https://www.srgresearch.com/articles/european-cloud-providers-local-market-share-now-holds-steady-at-15?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The Commission's 2026 Cloud and AI Development Act explicitly describes this dependence as a threat to European autonomy and resilience. CADA aims to at least triple EU data-centre capacity over the next 5–7 years and introduces a common European framework for assessing cloud and AI sovereignty. [Digital Strategy](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cloud-and-ai-development-act?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

But EProject should **not build data centres**. That would violate the low-capital requirement.

Instead, the more interesting startup opportunity emerges because Europe is now creating the underlying infrastructure—European clouds, AI Factories, HPC centres, sovereign infrastructure—but that infrastructure is fragmented.

The opportunity is therefore the **software/control layer above European infrastructure**.

Conceptually:

**OVH / Scaleway / T-Systems / national clouds / private infrastructure / EuroHPC / AI Factories / edge computing**

↓

**European orchestration, deployment, model/runtime, portability and sovereignty layer**

↓

**European enterprises and governments**

Investigation areas might eventually include federated inference, workload placement, sovereign AI deployment, model routing, cross-cloud orchestration, confidential computing, model governance or workload portability.

The strongest positioning would probably not be “avoid AWS because America is bad.” That is commercially weak.

It would instead be:

> **Run AI workloads where cost, latency, sovereignty, security and regulation make the most sense—without becoming locked into one hyperscaler.**

That can sell even to customers that continue using AWS or Azure.

The moat could become strong through cloud integrations, orchestration data, policy engines, European certification, switching infrastructure and an ecosystem around open-source components.

CADA is particularly significant because the EU now proposes four formal sovereignty assurance levels for cloud services and a joint European public-sector procurement framework. That could create an entire software ecosystem around sovereignty assessment and workload placement. [Digital Strategy](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cloud-and-ai-development-act?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Main risk:** hyperscalers will aggressively respond with their own “sovereign cloud” offerings. EProject therefore needs to own a layer where cloud neutrality itself creates value.

**Viability: 8.9/10**  
**Confidence: 8.8/10**

# 4. European Grid & Energy Intelligence

**Thesis:** use AI to extract substantially more capacity, flexibility and reliability from Europe's electricity system.

Energy is becoming a computing problem as much as an infrastructure problem.

Europe needs an estimated **€584 billion of grid investment by 2030**. About 40% of Europe's distribution grids are already more than 40 years old. Looking further ahead, the Commission estimates approximately €730 billion of distribution-grid investment and €477 billion of transmission investment may be needed by 2040. [Energy](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/topics/infrastructure/european-grids_en?prefLang=sk\&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

But building new cables, substations and transformers is slow.

Software that allows Europe to make better use of existing infrastructure can therefore create enormous economic value.

The Commission has effectively validated this thesis. Its June 2026 **Strategic Roadmap for Digitalisation and AI in Energy** explicitly calls for European sovereign AI solutions for grid optimisation, industrial/building energy efficiency and demand-side flexibility. It has also launched the AI.grids initiative toward pan-European AI capabilities for power grids. [Energy](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/topics/eus-energy-system/digitalisation-energy-system_en?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That opens a large sector:

> **AI as an operating intelligence layer for Europe's increasingly decentralised electricity system.**

Potential areas for later investigation include congestion forecasting, grid connection planning, distributed energy coordination, battery optimisation, virtual power plants, industrial demand flexibility, renewable forecasting, grid maintenance and electricity-market optimisation.

The startup does **not** need to own batteries, power stations or grids. The attractive EProject version is capital-light software sitting on top of them.

This can create powerful moats because energy systems generate proprietary time-series and operational data, while algorithms improve with deployment. Grid and market integrations also raise switching costs.

Commercial models can include SaaS, revenue sharing on achieved savings/trading revenue, licences to utilities and recurring contracts with industrial customers.

There is already venture evidence that the category can support meaningful businesses: Europe now has a rapidly growing GridTech ecosystem covering flexibility platforms, battery optimisation, forecasting and network analytics. [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/charts/landscape-48597-grid-technology-infrastructure-startups-in-europe?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Main risk:** selling directly to grid operators can involve extremely long procurement cycles. A winning entry may therefore originate with industrial customers, aggregators or distributed energy assets before moving deeper into grid infrastructure.

**Viability: 8.7/10**  
**Confidence: 8.9/10**

# 5. European AI-Native Semiconductor Design Stack

**Thesis:** help Europe own more of the *design intelligence* behind semiconductors rather than attempting to manufacture chips.

This is the highest-risk project among the five, but perhaps the deepest moat.

Europe's semiconductor discussion often centres on fabs. Fabs require billions and are obviously incompatible with EProject.

But semiconductor sovereignty has another layer: **chip architecture, design, simulation, verification and EDA software**.

The Commission explicitly says the EU remains dependent on third countries for important parts of semiconductor design and manufacturing. Chips Act 2.0 therefore makes strengthening European design capabilities a central objective. [Digital Strategy](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/proposal-chips-act-20?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The Act proposes expanding a European virtual chip-design platform containing tools, IP and design assets, while supporting **open-source EDA tools** and European startups and SMEs using the platform. [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A52026PC0504\&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The Commission also estimates that roughly **€3–4 billion** of public and private investment could be mobilised specifically to foster European fabless chip-design companies. [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/competitiveness/competitiveness-coordination-tool-projects/advanced-chips_en?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That creates an interesting EProject thesis:

> **Use AI to reduce the cost, expertise and time needed to design, verify and optimise specialised European chips.**

The eventual product could lie somewhere within architecture optimisation, verification, simulation, physical design, hardware/software co-design, chiplets, edge-AI accelerators or open-source EDA—but choosing among those should be a separate investigation.

This sector has unusually strong moat potential. Good semiconductor tooling becomes embedded in engineering workflows, benefits from proprietary design knowledge and requires very specialised expertise to reproduce.

Licensing economics can also be excellent.

Chips JU provides a direct EU financing route and currently supports design platforms, AI hardware, edge computing and semiconductor ecosystem development. [Chips JU](https://www.chips-ju.europa.eu/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Why only #5?

Because the technical entry barrier is very high. You can start it with €1–3 million rather than €1–3 billion, but you still need an unusually strong semiconductor/ML founding team and the product must integrate into extremely conservative design flows.

**Viability: 8.1/10**  
**Confidence: 8.0/10**

# The common pattern

These five opportunities actually point toward a fairly coherent definition of what **EProject should be**.

I would avoid businesses whose principal moat is simply an LLM prompt, interface or short-lived model advantage. Foundation models will increasingly commoditise that layer.

Instead, EProject should search for:

**AI × strategically important European infrastructure × proprietary operational data × difficult integration × European regulation/standards.**

That combination produces a much stronger moat.

The most valuable European startup might therefore not invent another foundation model. It could take existing European and open models and embed intelligence deeply into **factories, grids, security systems, cloud infrastructure or semiconductor engineering**.

That approach also matches the EU's own evolving AI policy. The Apply AI Strategy explicitly concentrates on strategic sectors including manufacturing, robotics, energy, defence, healthcare, mobility and the public sector, while encouraging a “buy European” approach—particularly for public-sector and open-source AI. [Digital Strategy](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/apply-ai?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## What I would investigate next

I would **not select one of these five yet**.

The next phase should decompose each sector into perhaps **5–10 concrete structural problems** and score those problems rather than brainstorming products. For example, Industrial AI would be broken into inspection, robot adaptation, machine configuration, process optimisation, predictive maintenance, industrial knowledge, etc.

For every problem I would then investigate four things very rigorously: who currently pays for it and how much; why existing solutions fail; whether AI has recently changed the economics enough to create a startup opportunity; and what proprietary asset EProject would accumulate after five years that a new competitor could not easily reproduce.

That exercise may produce a somewhat counter-intuitive result: the best EProject may emerge from a **narrow problem inside one of these broad sectors**, but have a very large European platform hidden behind it.

My current strongest thesis is therefore **Industrial AI/robotics first, cyber-resilience second, sovereign cloud/AI third**. They offer the best combination of European strategic relevance, realistic bootstrap/seed entry, defensibility, recurring commercial revenue and credible EU-financing routes.
